Oto means sound.

A browser named after something you hear, made to feel quiet. Here's what Oto is, what it does, and why it exists.

What it is

A calm browser for Windows, with your phone along for the ride.

Oto is a free web browser built on Chromium, so websites, Chrome extensions and streaming services work the way you expect. Around that engine, everything is Oto's own: the sidebar, the spaces, the look, the blocker, the sync.

It's made by one person, in the open. All the code is public on GitHub, and every update is built and published there, so anyone can see exactly what they're installing.

What it does

The everyday things, done quietly.

  • Tabs on the sideA vertical sidebar with Essentials, pinned tabs and folders, so long titles still fit.
  • SpacesWork, school and music each get their own tabs, icon and even their own logins.
  • Oto ShieldAds, trackers and known scam sites are stopped before a page loads.
  • AvocadoA private window that keeps nothing once you close it.
  • Your things, everywhereSettings, bookmarks and passwords sync, encrypted on your PC with your password.
  • My phoneOto for Android links to your PC: see your phone's tabs and battery, send tabs both ways.
The name

音, said “oto”.

In Japanese, 音 means sound. It's Oto's symbol, at the top of the sidebar and on every page Oto makes. It's also why Oto can play its own small sounds, and why the idea underneath everything is the opposite of noise: a browser you can barely hear.

The logo

A white hole, drawn like a graph.

Oto's logo is the Vortex: rings of light spinning around a white hole. A black hole pulls everything in; a white hole only gives light out. Each ring is drawn from the same simple equation, so the logo looks like something plotted on paper.

x = r·cos t  ·  y = k·r·sin t
From Aza

I wanted a browser that was fast, looked clean, and gave me the freedom to customize almost everything. I tried other browsers, but none of them really had everything I wanted.

So I decided to make my own.

That's how Oto started. It's a browser I'm building not just for myself, but for anyone who wants something different.

I use Oto myself every day, and with every update, I try to make it better. My goal is simple: a fast, beautiful, customizable browser that respects your privacy, with no hidden surprises.

And because it's open source, anyone can look at the code and see how it works.
